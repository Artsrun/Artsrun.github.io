Emoticon string ≠ emoji

12 Sep 2026 · UTS #51 v17.0

Unicode encodes letters. The face is a sentence.

UTS #51 (9 Sep 2025) defines an emoticon as a series of text characters meant as a face or gesture — ;-), ^‿^, ಠ_ಠ. An emoji is a colorful pictograph: one character, or a listed sequence. The Unicode block named “Emoticons” (U+1F600–U+1F64F) is the second thing: eighty encoded pictographs, all of them emoji, full since Unicode 8.0.

Kaomoji (顔文字, face-mark) stays on the first side. There is no code point for ¯\_(ツ)_/¯. What is encoded is the stolen alphabet: ASCII punctuation, Kannada U+0CA0 ಠ, undertie U+203F ‿, vertical parenthesis U+FE35 ︵, box drawing, katakana. Composition is usage. Stability is social repetition.

Emoji collapses a face into a noun. Kaomoji keeps the syntax. That is why a flip can take a new patient — another face, not only a table — without a committee. 🤷 cannot grow a second table.

Presentation selectors U+FE0E / U+FE0F are the official analogue of the sunglasses clause: same character, two costumes. ZWJ sequences are one grapheme cluster (UAX #29). A kaomoji is the opposite — every parenthesis is its own cluster. Do not put both encodings in one raw field without a flag. Mapping ;-) to 😉 is translation, not identity.
